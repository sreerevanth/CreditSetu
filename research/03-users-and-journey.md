# 03 · Users and journey

## Primary user

**Micro-enterprise owners with steady but informal income.** The brief names tailors, fabricators, dairy units and SHG-run enterprises. What they share:

- Revenue is real and regular, and increasingly visible in UPI and bank statements
- No ITR, no audited accounts, often no GST registration
- Loan needs are small (₹50,000 to ₹10 lakh), mostly working capital or a machine
- Low confidence with forms and branch processes; many prefer WhatsApp and their own language
- Time-poor: every branch visit is a day of lost sales

### Persona: Sunita (illustrative)

> A composite based on the users in the brief. Not a real person.

- Runs a four-machine tailoring unit in a tier-3 town; two helpers
- 14 months of steady UPI inflows from customers, with peaks before festivals and weddings and a dip in the monsoon
- Pays her fabric supplier and rent on time, by UPI
- Needs about ₹3 lakh for two new machines and fabric stock before wedding season
- Has Aadhaar, PAN and a savings account. No ITR, no Udyam registration.
- Was told by an agent he could "arrange" a loan for a 12% cut. A local moneylender offers money today at around 30%.

**What she needs to hear:** which schemes she qualifies for and why, in plain words; what to fix first; and a file she can take to the bank that won't bounce back.

## Secondary users

| Stakeholder | Their pain | What CreditSetu gives them |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank branch / NBFC credit officer** | Incomplete files, repeated visits, no way to assess thin-file borrowers | A standard, complete loan file with a cash-flow summary and cited scheme fit |
| **District Industries Centre / scheme agency** | Low scheme uptake, staff overloaded with walk-ins | Pre-screened applicants who already know which scheme fits |
| **Business correspondent / field agent** | Paid per application but spends hours on paperwork | A tool to prepare files faster and correctly; a transparent alternative to informal agents |
| **SHG federation** | Members need credit; federation staff can't advise on every scheme | One assistant for all members, in their language |

## Today's journey vs. with CreditSetu

| Stage | Today | With CreditSetu |
|---|---|---|
| **Find out** | Hears about "a government loan" from a neighbour or an agent | Asks on WhatsApp; gets a list of schemes she fits, each with its reason and source |
| **Check fit** | Visits the branch; told she needs ITRs | Profile built from her bank or UPI statement; gaps named (for example "register on Udyam") |
| **Prepare** | Collects 15–20 documents over weeks; agent fills forms for a fee | Checklist of only what's missing; application and project report pre-filled |
| **Apply** | File returned as incomplete; repeat | Reviews and approves the file herself; submits through the lender or portal |
| **Follow up** | Calls and visits; no status visibility | Status tracking and reminders |
| **Outcome** | Often gives up and borrows at 24–36% | Formal loan at scheme rates, and a credit history to build on |

## Key moments to get right in the demo

1. **The "aha":** "You qualify for PM Vishwakarma, a 5% loan for tailors, which you'd never heard of." Show the cited rule.
2. **The honest no:** "Stand-Up India isn't a fit: it starts at ₹10 lakh and is for new units." Judges trust a system that says no with a reason.
3. **The fixable gap:** "Register on Udyam (free) to raise your readiness score." Shows the score is actionable.
4. **Control:** the consent screen at the start and the approval screen at the end.
